ENTERPRISE PROCUREMENT POLICY — APPROVED VENDOR LIST (AVL)
Policy ID: PROC-POL-001
Version: 3.2
Effective Date: January 1, 2024
Owner: VP Global Procurement

1. PURPOSE
This policy establishes the requirements and governance framework for the Approved Vendor List (AVL) used across all hardware procurement categories. Only AVL-approved suppliers may be used for production sourcing without executive exception.

2. AVL ELIGIBILITY CRITERIA
To qualify for AVL status, a supplier must satisfy all of the following:
(a) Completed supplier qualification audit with score >= 80%
(b) ISO 9001:2015 certification (mandatory)
(c) Industry-specific certifications as applicable (IATF 16949, AS9100, IPC standards)
(d) Financial stability assessment (D&B rating >= 3A)
(e) Minimum 12-month operational track record in the relevant component category
(f) Completed environmental and social compliance questionnaire
(g) Executed Master Supply Agreement with approved commercial terms

3. AVL STATUS CATEGORIES
- AVL Approved: Fully qualified for production sourcing
- AVL Conditional: Approved with restrictions (volume caps, product line limitations)
- AVL Probation: Under performance review; sourcing limited to non-critical components
- Non-AVL: Not approved for production sourcing

4. NON-AVL SOURCING EXCEPTIONS
Non-AVL suppliers may only be used under the following conditions:
(a) Emergency sourcing during declared supply disruptions (requires VP Procurement approval)
(b) Prototype and development sourcing (limited to 500 units)
(c) Dual-source qualification in progress (time-limited, max 6 months)
All Non-AVL sourcing requires documented justification and risk assessment.

5. AVL REVIEW CYCLE
AVL status is reviewed quarterly based on:
- On-time delivery performance
- Quality metrics (PPM defect rate)
- Responsiveness to capacity inquiries
- Contract compliance
- Financial health indicators
Suppliers failing two consecutive quarterly reviews are moved to AVL Probation.

6. AVL REVOCATION
AVL status is revoked immediately upon:
- Critical quality escape affecting end-customer products
- Material compliance violation (RoHS, REACH)
- Bankruptcy or insolvency filing
- Sanctioned entity designation
- Facility closure without transition plan
